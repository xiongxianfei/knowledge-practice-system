# Use KPS for one real task

## Key takeaway

Begin with a real question; retrieve or compose only the knowledge needed, use it, and record what happened.

## Summary

**Stage order:** Numbers show the recommended reading and working order, not a compulsory completion sequence. Enter the stage matching the present question while respecting its stated prerequisites. Revisit an earlier stage when new evidence warrants it. A stage number is not a stable identity.

KPS is a five-role knowledge system: Concepts define, Principles explain relationships, Models represent, Methods operate and Practices coordinate. References identify the external support; personal records contain actual use. This Practice turns one question into a bounded action and learning loop without requiring a complete library first.

## Stage1 Bound the task

**Summary**

**Goal:** Identify the outcome and what could make action inappropriate.

**Why:** A useful answer depends on the task, constraints and what is already known.

**What to do:** Write the question, intended result, important constraints and one unresolved assumption.

**What to observe:** Whether you are describing a need, selecting a solution or testing a claim.

**Success signal:** The next decision is explicit and small enough to examine.

**Next:** Retrieve and inspect the local explanation for relevant knowledge.

**Entry and understanding**

Identify the outcome and what could make action inappropriate. The boundary is the stated task, not a requirement to complete every stage of the repository. A useful answer depends on the task, constraints and what is already known.

**Procedure**

1. Write one sentence: I need to accomplish or understand X in context Y. Separate the goal from your preferred tool.
2. List the facts already known and mark assumptions as assumptions. State risks, authority and practical prerequisites that could prevent action.
3. Select a small finishable output: a decision record, explanation, prototype, comparison or revised file. Do not begin by migrating all notes.

**Decision points and fallback**

If the goal is unclear, clarify it before inventing Methods. If safety or authority is missing, restrict the task to study or seek appropriate help.

## Stage2 Retrieve and inspect the local explanation

**Summary**

**Goal:** Choose usable knowledge rather than a large reading list.

**Why:** A title or working link does not establish that an object answers the current question.

**What to do:** Read a candidate Practice or Method’s Summary, scope and procedure; inspect deeper support only where needed.

**What to observe:** Missing terms, missing rationale and a mismatch between the source task and this one.

**Success signal:** You can explain what to do and why without opening every dependency.

**Next:** Use and observe for action, or build a small object when no useful one exists.

**Entry and understanding**

Choose usable knowledge rather than a large reading list. The boundary is the stated task, not a requirement to complete every stage of the repository. A title or working link does not establish that an object answers the current question.

**Procedure**

1. Find a Practice by goal or a Method by operation. A Principle supplies reusable explanatory reasoning, not a compulsory instruction.
2. Restate the chosen rationale in your own words: supported premise, local assumption and expected effect. Mark a gap rather than supplying a confident guess.
3. If there is no usable object, write the immediate operation and limits locally. Promote it into reusable knowledge only if its independent use is clear.
4. Open a Reference when an important claim needs checking. Identify the exact passage, scope and access extent; a respected source name is insufficient.

**Decision points and fallback**

If the file is merely a link hub, add the locally missing explanation or record it as unsuitable. Do not mistake fluent text for verified support.

## Stage3 Use and observe

**Summary**

**Goal:** Carry out one bounded operation and retain the actual result.

**Why:** An expected outcome and an observed outcome have different evidentiary roles.

**What to do:** Predict one signal, perform the safe task and compare the result with the expectation.

**What to observe:** Whether the intended change happened and what else changed.

**Success signal:** A result or an honest inconclusive outcome is recorded.

**Next:** Update the smallest justified target for a provisional decision.

**Entry and understanding**

Carry out one bounded operation and retain the actual result. The boundary is the stated task, not a requirement to complete every stage of the repository. An expected outcome and an observed outcome have different evidentiary roles.

**Procedure**

1. State the action, expected signal and a condition that would count against it. Specify the version of the Method or Practice used.
2. Perform only the authorised, safe task. Preserve actual inputs or observations, not an improved story reconstructed after the result.
3. Compare the outcome with the expectation. Record confounding differences such as task difficulty, order, effort or missing measurements.

**Decision points and fallback**

If the procedure cannot be executed, classify the gap before calling the idea false. If results are noisy or contrary, retain the uncertainty.

## Stage4 Update the smallest justified target

**Summary**

**Goal:** Convert experience into a proportionate revision.

**Why:** One result can guide a local choice without establishing a universal relationship.

**What to do:** Choose keep, modify, discard, unresolved or seek more evidence; revise the affected content only.

**What to observe:** Whether you are changing a fact, interpretation, procedure or local preference.

**Success signal:** The revision and its reason are traceable without overwriting the original observation.

**Next:** Return to the next real task or review later.

**Entry and understanding**

Convert experience into a proportionate revision. The boundary is the stated task, not a requirement to complete every stage of the repository. One result can guide a local choice without establishing a universal relationship.

**Procedure**

1. Write a concise record: question, action, observation, interpretation, decision and next check. No record is required when there is no meaningful new information.
2. Update the relevant Method, Practice step, scope or explanatory object. Do not generalize a single successful run into an unqualified Principle.
3. Inspect files that summarize the changed object, preserving their local completeness and qualifiers. Refresh review fingerprints only after inspection.

**Decision points and fallback**

An outcome that does not distinguish competing explanations should remain unresolved. Not every session justifies a new file or a version change.

## Troubleshooting

If the object is understandable only after opening links, add the minimum missing meaning locally. If it becomes huge, separate general depth from the task-specific summary. If sources disagree, preserve the disagreement and narrow the claim. If the validator passes but the task remains unclear, revise the prose; a structural pass is not a usability result.

## Local evidence and authorship

The stages are authored KPS procedures, not an independently evaluated productivity programme. Source synthesis borrows the distinction between studies and reports; provenance supplies origin information rather than truth; empirical learning comparisons apply only to their actual tasks and measures. The five-role organization and local-completeness contract remain explicit design choices.
