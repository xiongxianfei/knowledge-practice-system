# Review actual use and learn

## Key takeaway

Preserve the observation, challenge the interpretation, and revise only what the evidence warrants.

## Summary

**Stage order:** Numbers show the recommended reading and working order, not a compulsory completion sequence. Enter the stage matching the present question while respecting its stated prerequisites. Revisit an earlier stage when new evidence warrants it. A stage number is not a stable identity.

This Practice turns a meaningful application into a proportionate knowledge revision. It can be used after success, failure or a surprising result. It separates expected from actual outcomes, compares causal explanations and turns a proposed lesson into a future check rather than a universal rule.

## Stage1 Reconstruct the record

**Summary**

**Goal:** Establish what happened and what was known at the time.

**Why:** Later knowledge can make earlier choices look falsely obvious.

**What to do:** Gather the actual task, version, decisions, observations and missing data.

**What to observe:** Which statements are measured, reported, inferred or unknown.

**Success signal:** A factual account can be read without accepting one causal story.

**Next:** Compare explanations for explanation.

**Entry and understanding**

Establish what happened and what was known at the time. The boundary is the stated task, not a requirement to complete every stage of the repository. Later knowledge can make earlier choices look falsely obvious.

**Procedure**

1. Write the expected result and actual result. Include relevant context, inputs and the Method/Practice revision used.
2. Preserve information available before the choice separately from what became known later.
3. Keep source observations unchanged except for explicit corrections. Missing records remain missing.

**Decision points and fallback**

Do not invent a clean sequence to make the outcome easier to explain.

## Stage2 Compare explanations

**Summary**

**Goal:** Identify what the result does and does not establish.

**Why:** The same observation can be compatible with more than one mechanism.

**What to do:** Propose a primary explanation, an alternative and a discriminating observation.

**What to observe:** Implementation failures, task differences, chance and incomplete measurements.

**Success signal:** The interpretation has a scope and acknowledged uncertainty.

**Next:** Select the smallest warranted revision for a bounded lesson.

**Entry and understanding**

Identify what the result does and does not establish. The boundary is the stated task, not a requirement to complete every stage of the repository. The same observation can be compatible with more than one mechanism.

**Procedure**

1. Check whether the intended operation was actually performed. A failed implementation is not the same as a failed explanatory Model.
2. List contributors and interactions without forcing one root cause. Use a Model when a structured explanation helps.
3. Ask what plausible change would distinguish explanations. A counterfactual is a reasoning tool, not an observed experimental result.

**Decision points and fallback**

If available evidence cannot distinguish causes, keep the result descriptive and choose a modest next test.

## Stage3 Select the smallest warranted revision

**Summary**

**Goal:** Translate the evidence into useful learning without overgeneralization.

**Why:** A local outcome can justify a local adaptation without changing the whole theory.

**What to do:** Choose a change to a cue, procedure, scope, Model or explanatory relationship.

**What to observe:** Whether the conclusion extends beyond the observed context.

**Success signal:** The revision identifies both its basis and its limit.

**Next:** Define the next check for checking and follow-through.

**Entry and understanding**

Translate the evidence into useful learning without overgeneralization. The boundary is the stated task, not a requirement to complete every stage of the repository. A local outcome can justify a local adaptation without changing the whole theory.

**Procedure**

1. Write the lesson as a conditional statement. Keep a local instruction in a Method or Practice unless independent reuse justifies a Principle.
2. Preserve useful observations even when the original explanation was wrong. Replace the explanation rather than erasing the evidence.
3. Identify dependent summaries that need review and decide whether the update changes interpretation or use enough to warrant a version.

**Decision points and fallback**

When evidence is weak, update confidence or the next question rather than announcing a general truth.

## Stage4 Define the next check

**Summary**

**Goal:** Keep learning connected to future evidence.

**Why:** A plausible lesson needs opportunities to be challenged.

**What to do:** Specify a safe observable test, review trigger or follow-up condition.

**What to observe:** What would count against the revised explanation.

**Success signal:** The next action and unresolved issues are explicit.

**Next:** Return to the relevant real task.

**Entry and understanding**

Keep learning connected to future evidence. The boundary is the stated task, not a requirement to complete every stage of the repository. A plausible lesson needs opportunities to be challenged.

**Procedure**

1. State the expected signal, important controls and stopping conditions. For consequential decisions, identify the appropriate expertise or approval.
2. Check the revised object with links hidden, then compare its summary dependencies for consistency.
3. Record whether the next check has occurred. A plan remains a plan until actual observations are available.

**Decision points and fallback**

No feasible discriminating test does not force certainty. Preserve the uncertainty and choose the least misleading representation.

## Troubleshooting

If the object is understandable only after opening links, add the minimum missing meaning locally. If it becomes huge, separate general depth from the task-specific summary. If sources disagree, preserve the disagreement and narrow the claim. If the validator passes but the task remains unclear, revise the prose; a structural pass is not a usability result.

## Local evidence and authorship

The stages are authored KPS procedures, not an independently evaluated productivity programme. Source synthesis borrows the distinction between studies and reports; provenance supplies origin information rather than truth; empirical learning comparisons apply only to their actual tasks and measures. The five-role organization and local-completeness contract remain explicit design choices.
