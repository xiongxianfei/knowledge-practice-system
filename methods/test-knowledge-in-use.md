# Test knowledge in use

## Key takeaway

Assess whether an object helps the actual goal: recall, retrieval, decision or execution.

## Summary

Immediate familiarity and success on one measure need not establish later recall or task performance. The evaluation has to match the goal. Choose one low-stakes real task and predict a useful signal before reading the result. Compare actual execution with the intended procedure. Keep any observed benefit separate from a claim that the causal explanation is proven.

## Inputs and prerequisites

A concrete question, the material or actual record to inspect, and enough context to distinguish observed information from a proposed interpretation. No result is assumed in advance.

## Local explanatory basis

The effect attributed to a learning activity depends on the learning task, comparison activities and later test. An observed improvement can be compatible with several causal explanations when relevant conditions changed together.

## Objective
Assess whether an object helps the actual goal: recall, retrieval, decision or execution.

## Rationale
Immediate familiarity and success on one measure need not establish later recall or task performance. The evaluation has to match the goal.

## Evidence and reasoning
[Spaced retrieval: absolute spacing enhances learning regardless of relative spacing](../references/karpicke-2011-spaced-retrieval.md#absolute-and-relative-retrieval-spacing) informs a scoped recall option; [Re-examining the testing effect as a learning strategy: the advantage of retrieval practice over concept mapping as a methodological artifact](../references/mayrhofer-2023-reexamining-the-testing-effect.md#verbatim-performance-under-the-revised-comparison) warns against confounded comparisons. Application to KPS tasks is our inference.

## Steps
1. Choose a low-risk realistic task and define what success would look like before using the object.
2. For recall, attempt retrieval without the text, then check and correct it. For application, carry out or explain a new example and assess the actual result.
3. Compare an alternative only when conditions can be reasonably documented. Record exposure, time, order and assistance rather than pretending they were controlled.
4. Recheck after a useful interval or on a different case. Do not impose a universal schedule from one study.
5. Record what the object helped, what it omitted and which conclusion remains unsupported.

## Expected effect
A bounded result informing revision, not a universal efficacy claim.

## Validation and counterevidence
Wrong answers, failure on a new case, reliance on hidden assistance or inconsistent later performance can weaken the proposed benefit.

## Limits
Do not experimentally expose people to danger or use this as a substitute for regulated validation.

## Local evidence summary


[Spaced retrieval: absolute spacing enhances learning regardless of relative spacing](../references/karpicke-2011-spaced-retrieval.md#absolute-and-relative-retrieval-spacing) contributes: In the reported experiment, expanding the total spacing between repeated retrieval attempts improved later retention; no relative spacing schedule was inherently superior in that comparison.

[Re-examining the testing effect as a learning strategy: the advantage of retrieval practice over concept mapping as a methodological artifact](../references/mayrhofer-2023-reexamining-the-testing-effect.md#verbatim-performance-under-the-revised-comparison) contributes: The study reports no statistically significant retrieval advantage on verbatim questions once additional memorization is included in the mapping comparison. This is a condition-specific result.

Citation identifies support for the stated claim and scope. Any further causal or practical inference remains separately reviewable.
