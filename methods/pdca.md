# Plan Do Check Act

## Key takeaway

Use a bounded trial to inform the next improvement decision.

## Summary

ASQ describes PDCA as an iterative improvement model with an executable procedure. KPS classifies that procedural use as a Method.

## Inputs and prerequisites

An improvement opportunity, a process owner, a small permitted trial and a criterion that can distinguish useful change from activity alone.

## Rationale

Acting on the result of a bounded test is different from assuming the plan succeeded.

## Procedure

1. Plan the change and what the trial should clarify.
2. Do a small-scale test under the agreed conditions.
3. Check the result and what was learned.
4. Act on that learning: adopt an appropriate change or revise the plan and repeat.

## Synthetic example

A team proposes a clearer form label, tests one scenario, and finds that users still choose the wrong field. The appropriate next action can be a different design rather than wider rollout. No trial occurred in this example.

## Output and validation

Record the intended change, actual trial, result and consequent action. Check that the action follows the observed result, not merely the original preference.

## Limits

PDCA does not specify a statistical design or demonstrate a causal effect by itself. One successful test does not justify unrestricted generalization. This summary is neither an ISO certification procedure nor evidence that KPS is effective.
