# PICO Question Formulation

## Key takeaway

Define the comparison before searching for intervention evidence.

## Summary

This presents PICO in its intervention-review scope; engineering substitutions belong in the receiving Practice.

## Inputs and prerequisites

An intervention question, intended users of the answer and enough domain knowledge to identify meaningful outcomes. For a definition or syntax question, PICO is usually unnecessary.

## Rationale

Different populations, comparators or outcomes can lead to different searches and conclusions.

## Procedure

1. Specify the population.
2. Specify the intervention and comparator independently.
3. Specify the relevant outcomes and their assessment timing.
4. Distinguish the question intended for the review from what each included study actually investigated.
5. Resolve material ambiguity before selecting or combining evidence.

## Worked illustration

A fictional review compares intervention A with usual care in population P for functioning at time T. “A is better” is insufficient until P, the comparison and functioning are operationalized. No clinical recommendation is implied.

## Output and validation

Produce an explicit P/I/C/O question and note unresolved scope choices. Check that plausible different answers would matter to the decision and that included studies match the intended comparison.

## Limits

This is not a complete systematic-review protocol or a universal format for every knowledge question. The original PICO meanings are retained; replacing population with a software platform would be an explicitly labelled adaptation.
