# Evidence applicability depends on the question

## Key takeaway

Evidence for one population, task, comparison or outcome does not automatically establish an effect for a materially different target.

## Summary

Transfer adds premises: the mechanism must remain relevant, the intervention sufficiently similar, and the target outcome actually related to what was measured.

## Worked use and boundary

A study of delayed text recall can inform a review exercise. It does not establish that the same exercise improves architectural decisions. The new outcome and task require an explicit additional argument or check.

## Statement
Evidence for one population, task, comparison or outcome does not automatically establish an effect for a materially different target.

This is methodological guidance plus a separate empirical example.

## Derivation and applications
A Method can be a reasonable candidate when evidence is indirect, provided the missing bridge is explicit and the proposed use has suitable checks. No mandatory count or universal source ranking is implied.

## Scope
Generalization from research, standards, expert instructions or another user’s experience.

## Limits and alternatives
A definition may have one authoritative source; a formal consequence may require reasoning rather than several experiments. Similarity alone is not a guarantee either.

## Qualification and challenge
Direct target-context studies or a sound argument establishing the relevant invariances can strengthen the bridge.
