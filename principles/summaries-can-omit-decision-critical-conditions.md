# Summaries can omit decision critical conditions

## Key takeaway

A summary can retain a conclusion while dropping a condition that changes its legitimate use.

## Summary

Compression selects information. If an omitted condition affects whether an action is appropriate, the shorter account may support a different decision from the full account. This conditional relationship explains why local summaries need scope and safeguards, not merely shorter wording.

## Statement

A summary that omits a condition material to interpreting or applying a claim can change the decision that the claim appears to support.

## Explanation

Suppose a Method permits a short swimming comparison only when both alternatives are comfortable and air access is dependable. “Compare A and B” preserves the operation but loses its entry constraint. The unqualified summary is not operationally equivalent even though its main verbs match.

## Basis and derivation

This is a representational argument: the premise includes a condition; a restatement without that condition permits a broader reading. No empirical incidence rate or learning advantage is asserted. It supports inspecting safeguards at every level of compression.

## Applications and alternatives

A stage may repeat the essential stop condition, restrict its declared scope, or embed a compact task-specific precondition. Full duplication is another possible response but introduces maintenance costs. There is no claim that every detail is equally important.

## Scope and limits

Applies when omitted information changes a relevant interpretation or decision. A detail unrelated to the local task can legitimately remain at the canonical source. The authoring choice requires judgement and reader testing; a checksum cannot identify which omission matters.
