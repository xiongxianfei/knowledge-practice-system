# Testing Effect

## Key takeaway

Retrieval can change later retention rather than merely measure it.

## Summary

The testing effect is an empirical learning relationship. Its magnitude and applicability depend on the task, comparator and assessment.

## Statement

In the inspected learning experiments, retrieval practice supported later performance under the reported conditions. This is not a rule that testing always beats every study activity.

## Explanation and boundary

Karpicke and Blunt compared retrieval and concept-mapping activities on science-text learning. Their proposed retrieval-based explanation and their observed comparison should not be treated as identical kinds of support. A later re-examination changed aspects of the activity comparison and obtained outcome-specific results.

## Example of a permissible inference

A learner could investigate whether recalling a short explanation helps later retention. That is a candidate application, not evidence that a folder layout works or that motor skills improve. Actual retrieval errors, delay and the later task would have to be recorded.

## Limits

No mechanism is declared uniquely proven. Existing source records preserve the disagreement and inspection history. A local learning preference remains in a Practice or private record.
