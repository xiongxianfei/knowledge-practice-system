# Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions version 6 5

## Key takeaway

This chapter explains how review authors handle reports and study information.

## Summary

This chapter explains how review authors handle reports and study information. It is used here to distinguish multiple publications from independent studies and to keep extraction linked to the claim. Borrowing that distinction does not make a KPS desk comparison a Cochrane systematic review.


## Current inspection and use limit

Chapter 5 on studies versus reports and chapter 14 on evidence interpretation were inspected online. They are one handbook family, and the KPS synthesis procedure is an adaptation rather than a Cochrane review.

## Reports and underlying studies

Several reports can describe one underlying study; report count is therefore not study count.

**Locator:** Chapter 5, section 5.2.1

## Indirectness relative to the target question

Evidence may be indirect when study populations, interventions, comparisons or outcomes differ from the target question.

**Locator:** [Chapter 14, section 14.2.2](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-14), Indirectness of evidence

## What this source does not establish

These are systematic-review methods, not empirical proof that KPS or a personal swimming comparison works. This package is not a Cochrane review, formal GRADE assessment or exhaustive search.
