# Retrieval practice produces more learning than elaborative studying with concept mapping

## Key takeaway

Two experiments reported an advantage for the tested retrieval conditions over elaborative mapping on specified delayed learning measures.

## Summary

Two experiments reported an advantage for the tested retrieval conditions over elaborative mapping on specified delayed learning measures. Total learning time was matched, but the activities differed. This is evidence about those comparisons, not proof that mapping is generally ineffective or that KPS improves decisions.


## Current inspection and use limit

## Reported learning comparison

The reported retrieval condition outperformed the concept-mapping condition on the study’s learning outcomes.

**Locator:** Main article pp. 773–774, results for Experiments 1 and 2.

## Matched total time and different activities

Experiment 1 compared mapping with the text present against retrieval with restudy. The main article explicitly states that total learning time was matched.

**Locator:** Main article p. 773, left column; experiment 2 p. 774.

## What this source does not establish

A particular comparison does not prove universal superiority of retrieval over mapping. The comparator design is challenged by the Mayrhofer study.

The later methodological challenge concerns the distribution of learning activities, not a claim that the original main article failed to match total learning time. Neither paper evaluates this KPS repository.
