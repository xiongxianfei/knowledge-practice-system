# KPS Body of Knowledge Outline

## Key takeaway

Understand, reconstruct, learn, apply and develop knowledge for a concrete purpose. Use established subject structures to identify required understanding and capability, then select the explanations and Practices that support the task.

## Summary

This outline defines KPS's selected body of knowledge across seven areas and 34 topics: knowledge and understanding; inquiry and evidence; representation and organization; synthesis and communication; learning and capability; practical reasoning and action; and experience and knowledge development. These areas draw on established accounts from several disciplines. Their grouping is an authored selection for KPS, not a universal taxonomy or a sequence of construction stages.

Core currently provides 20 knowledge objects and 22 Reference records. These support parts of the outline; they do not complete its wider scope. The tables distinguish required knowledge, intended capabilities and remaining gaps. Concepts, Principles, Models, Methods and Practices remain document roles; one explanation may support several topics.

## Purpose and scope

**Target:** help a reader explain a bounded problem, reconstruct relevant knowledge faithfully, apply it to an appropriate task, and use observations to improve understanding or investigate a new idea.

The audience includes learners, practitioners and authors of domain knowledge packages. Entry requires a concrete question and access to relevant material; specialised domain prerequisites still govern actual application. Intended depth is working understanding: explain a distinction, interpret a representation, compare accounts, choose an operation and justify a bounded next step. Readers need not study every area before beginning a supported Practice.

This is a selected foundation, not a complete philosophy curriculum, research-methods handbook, theory of learning or professional qualification. Formal logic, statistical inference, causal identification, advanced experimental design and domain-specific operations require further sources and expertise. The [KPS application guidance](#applying-the-knowledge-through-kps) below is an authoring branch for people building packages; it does not define the subject divisions.

## Reading the coverage tables

**Understand** identifies required meanings, relationships and limits. **Be able to do** states a bounded capability at the intended depth. These are development goals, not observed learner results or a prescription to perform an unsupported task.

**Partial** means existing local material contributes but leaves a stated gap. **Source only** means a relevant external contribution is recorded without a dedicated local explanation. **Missing** means focused explanation and source development are still needed. No broad topic below is claimed complete merely because a relevant file exists. Practices own detailed actions, prerequisites, interpretation and fallback choices.

Use the [Practice targets](#practice-targets) to choose an application and the [development priorities](#development-priorities) to identify useful next work. Subject coverage is broader than the work needed for any one task.

## Knowledge and understanding

The starting questions concern knowledge, belief and understanding, including their relationships to reasons and ability. The [epistemology account](../references/steup-neta-2024-epistemology.md) supplies selected distinctions and identifies disagreements; this outline does not settle them.

| Topic | Understand — required knowledge | Be able to do — intended depth | Existing explanations and gaps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knowledge belief and justification | Knowledge claims, belief, justification and competing accounts of their relationship | Explain what supports a selected claim and why belief or justification alone does not settle every question about knowledge. | **Source only:** [Epistemology](../references/steup-neta-2024-epistemology.md), factual knowledge and justification; no dedicated Core explanation yet. |
| Knowing that and knowing how | Factual knowledge, practical ability and disputed accounts of their relationship | Distinguish explaining a task from carrying it out, without treating that distinction as a settled philosophical theory. | **Source only:** [Epistemology](../references/steup-neta-2024-epistemology.md), section 2.2 and its competing accounts. |
| Understanding and explanation | Explanatory relationships, mechanisms, assumptions and the difference between recall and understanding | Explain a bounded case in one’s own words and identify which relationship or assumption a changed case would challenge. | **Missing:** no dedicated explanation yet; further sources needed. |
| Uncertainty and limits | Unknowns, applicability limits and the difference between an unsupported claim and a supported bounded claim | State the relevant uncertainty and conditions that prevent applying a claim to the selected task. | **Partial:** [Evidence Indirectness](../concepts/evidence-indirectness.md) covers one applicability question; broader uncertainty remains a gap. |

## Knowledge representation and organization

This area concerns expressing meanings and making their relationships navigable. The adopted SKOS subset concerns vocabularies; the concept-map account concerns propositions and explicit conceptual links. Their relationships should remain distinguishable.

| Topic | Understand — required knowledge | Be able to do — intended depth | Existing explanations and gaps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Concepts terms and definitions | Meaning versus labels, definitions, identifiers and scope | Define a term for a selected context and distinguish its meaning, label and identifier. | **Partial:** [SKOS Concept](../concepts/skos-concept.md); [URI](../concepts/uniform-resource-identifier.md) for identifiers. Broader concept and definition theories remain gaps. |
| Propositions and relationships | Statements formed from concepts and explicit relations; semantic versus causal relationships | Write and interpret a small set of labeled relationships, preserving their direction and stated meaning. | **Partial:** [SKOS Data Model](../models/skos-data-model.md) for vocabulary relations. Source only: [Novak and Cañas](../references/novak-canas-2008-concept-maps.md) for propositions and conceptual links. |
| Categories and classification | Grouping criteria, hierarchy, association and classification boundaries | Justify a grouping and distinguish a broader or narrower relation from an associative connection. | **Partial:** [SKOS Data Model](../models/skos-data-model.md) — vocabulary hierarchy and association; broader classification theory needs coverage. |
| Models and representations | Purpose, entities, attributes, relationships, assumptions and limits of a representation | Use a small representation to explain a case and identify a conclusion the model does not support. | **Partial:** [SKOS](../models/skos-data-model.md) and [PROV](../models/prov-data-model.md) are specific examples. A general account of representation and causal models remains a gap. |
| Subject structures and mappings | Established disciplinary subdivisions, context-dependent organization and mappings between vocabularies | Select a sourced outline for a target and explain where two accounts correspond, differ or cannot be equated. | **Partial:** [SKOS Data Model](../models/skos-data-model.md) — mappings between vocabularies. ## Knowledge synthesis and communication

Organization describes a structure of meanings; synthesis considers how selected accounts can contribute to an explanation. Communication concerns making that explanation intelligible to an audience. These topics overlap without requiring every account to be merged.

| Topic | Understand — required knowledge | Be able to do — intended depth | Existing explanations and gaps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Comparing accounts | Agreement, scope differences, incompatible assumptions and vocabulary correspondences | Compare two located accounts and distinguish actual disagreement from a difference of meaning or scope. | **Partial:** [SKOS mappings](../models/skos-data-model.md) cover vocabulary correspondences. General comparative synthesis remains a gap. |
| Selecting relevant knowledge | Purpose, audience, prerequisites and relevance of an explanation to a question | Select the distinctions and supporting knowledge needed for a bounded Practice and explain the selection. | **Partial:** Local application: [Build domain knowledge](../practices/build-domain-knowledge.md). Source only: [concept-map focus questions](../references/novak-canas-2008-concept-maps.md). The outline authoring guide adds a KPS application; general relevance-selection explanations remain incomplete. |
| Integrating explanations | How source contributions connect while retaining their conditions and attribution | Write a coherent explanation connecting selected contributions, with local inferences identified and source limits preserved. | **Partial:** Local application: [Build domain knowledge](../practices/build-domain-knowledge.md). No general synthesis method yet. |
| Assumptions and disagreement | Conditions, competing interpretations and unresolved evidence | Keep a material disagreement visible and state how it affects the proposed explanation or action. | **Partial:** Local application: [Build domain knowledge](../practices/build-domain-knowledge.md). | Examples analogies and presentation | Worked cases, audience needs, explanatory analogies and their boundaries | Construct a clearly labeled example and explain where its comparison or analogy stops applying. | **Source only:** [How People Learn II](../references/national-academies-2018-knowledge-and-reasoning.md) for selected explanatory activities; [Diataxis](../references/procida-undated-how-to-guides.md) for task-oriented writing. | Problem formulation | Observed difficulty, proposed explanation and the decision actually required | Bound a practical problem, separate symptom from hypothesis and define a useful intended outcome. | **Partial:** Local application: [Use KPS](../practices/use-kps.md). | Judgment and decision making | Reasons for alternatives, uncertainty, values and context | Compare plausible actions, state reasons for a choice and identify information that could change it. | **Source only:** [Practical Reason](../references/wallace-kiesewetter-2024-practical-reason.md). ## Experience and knowledge development

This area connects what happens during an activity with possible changes in understanding and action. It includes improvement within a situation and inquiry that might support broader conclusions.

| Topic | Understand — required knowledge | Be able to do — intended depth | Existing explanations and gaps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interpreting outcomes | Recorded actions and observations, competing explanations and causal-attribution limits | Reconstruct a task record and distinguish what happened from the explanation proposed for it. | **Partial:** Local application: [Review and learn](../practices/review-and-learn.md); [Provenance](../concepts/provenance.md) supports reconstructing the record, not establishing its cause. |
| Reflection and error correction | Reviewing interpretations, identifying errors and selecting proportionate corrections | Identify a warranted revision from an actual record, or label the exercise as hypothetical when no record exists. | **Partial:** [PDCA](../methods/pdca.md) covers improvement; [Review and learn](../practices/review-and-learn.md) supplies local guidance. | Revising explanations | How new evidence bears on assumptions, relationships and competing accounts | Identify which claim or assumption new information challenges and propose the smallest justified revision. | **Partial:** Local application: [Review and learn](../practices/review-and-learn.md). | Experimentation improvement and new ideas | Improvement questions, hypotheses, comparisons, observations and limits of local trials | Propose a bounded investigation with an expected observation and an alternative explanation; separate an idea from a demonstrated improvement. | **Partial:** [PDCA](../methods/pdca.md); local application: [Improve KPS](../practices/improve-kps.md). ## Connections across subjects

These are relationships to investigate and use, not a fixed processing or learning sequence.

| Connection | Relationship to follow |
|---|---|
| Knowledge and evidence | A claim to knowledge raises questions about its support; evidence-handling procedures address only part of that question. |
| Evidence and models | Observations and arguments can support or challenge a representation within its assumptions. |
| Organization and synthesis | Explicit distinctions help compare accounts without silently treating different meanings as equivalent. |
| Communication and learning | An explanation supplies material for learning; the learner's grasp of its relationships is a separate question. |
| Learning and action | Transfer concerns using learning in another situation; recalling a statement and performing a task are different targets. |
| Action and experience | A chosen action produces outcomes to interpret, alongside the conditions in which it was performed. |
| Experience and inquiry | An unexpected outcome can motivate a new question or a revised explanation. |

For example, an English learner might recall a sound rule but struggle to analyze a recording. This hypothetical case connects representation, learning, transfer and evidence: the recording's context, the explanation's limits and the learner's activity all become relevant questions. The English package supplies the linguistic distinctions; a learning Practice supplies the analysis activity. No outcome is claimed for this example.

## Development priorities

Prioritize missing support for the target Practices rather than the number of empty topic rows. The following are proposed content-development tasks, not new completed Methods or Practices.

1. **Inference and argument:** explain premises, conclusions, assumptions and alternatives around a bounded claim. This supports comparing sources in Build domain knowledge and interpreting outcomes in Review and learn. Inspect appropriate sources before writing the explanation.
2. **Comparing and integrating accounts:** develop a worked comparison that preserves differences in meaning, scope and evidence, then shows a justified local synthesis. Support it with the first priority's reasoning account; extend the existing construction Practice rather than creating a duplicate workflow.
3. **Observation, feedback and transfer:** explain what a task record measures and what an unfamiliar application can reveal. Develop source-supported examples for Use KPS and Review and learn; keep actual observations distinct from synthetic cases.
4. **Judgment and contextual adaptation:** develop bounded decision examples showing alternatives, values, constraints and the limits of adapting a Method. Broader coordination and experimental-design coverage remain visible future work.

## Maintaining the outline

Use the [role index](library.md) to find documents and its [source-to-application table](library.md#from-sources-to-kps-applications) to trace selected uses. This outline is the single subject coverage guide; Practices own activity sequences and the authoring contract owns format conventions.

Keep necessary topics visible before explanations exist. Update coverage notes when material addresses a requirement at its stated depth, and revise the overall illustration when its area structure changes. Preserve source disagreements, actual inspection limits and the difference between a proposed capability and demonstrated learning. No generator or additional coverage database is needed.
